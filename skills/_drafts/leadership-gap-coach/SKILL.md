<!-- reflection reason: Repeatable multi-step workflow: recall career context, research the technical-to-leadership transition, produce a personalized gap analysis plus development plan, and save progress to memory. The user is on an ongoing leadership-development journey, so this will recur. -->

1. Gather the user's career context: current role, years of experience, technical vs people leadership background, target roles, and any books/resources they've consumed on leadership or influence. Check memory for prior career context (target roles, resume location, skill gaps already discussed).
2. Run 1-2 search_and_synthesize queries on the specific transition relevant to the user (e.g., 'why technical experts struggle in leadership roles', 'key leadership skills for director-level roles, emotional intelligence, influence, stakeholder management') to ground the analysis in current research with citations.
3. Validate the user's self-assessment explicitly — confirm what they got right, add nuance, and correct any misconceptions (e.g., books on persuasion cover reading/influencing people but not the full leadership skill set).
4. Produce a personalized gap analysis: map the researched leadership skill pillars (identity shift doer→enabler, delegation, influencing without authority, executive communication/translation, emotional intelligence, strategic/business acumen, political/stakeholder navigation) against the user's specific profile and predicted blind spots.
5. Deliver a concrete development plan: for each gap give (a) why it matters for their target role, (b) 2-3 practical exercises or habits, (c) signals/proof points they can later put on their resume.
6. Recommend 2-3 next books or resources targeted at their specific gaps (not generic lists).
7. Persist durable facts to memory: target roles, identified gaps, and development focus areas so future conversations can build on the plan.
8. Offer a follow-up deep-dive on one pillar (e.g., stakeholder mapping in their current role or a delegation practice framework).
